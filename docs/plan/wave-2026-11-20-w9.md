## Where the plan goes against the verb and the stories

The verb puts story:canon-mutate one wave before story:protocol-floor and
story:revision-ordering, because story:canon-mutate and story:protocol-floor share
`crates/canon-cli/src/lib.rs`, `crates/canon-cli/src/main.rs`, `crates/canon/src/lib.rs` and
`ess/domains/cli.yaml`. Both stories say in § Order that they run in different waves for that
reason. The plan runs the three in one wave (plan page § Replan 9), and this is how the shared
files are kept apart:

- story:protocol-floor was built in wave 3 (`f2d7d8f7`, on base `be2c8613`) and neither reviewed
  nor merged. Its unit first merges the wave branch into its branch and repairs what the five
  waves since then broke; then its adversary pass runs; it merges first.
- story:canon-mutate is built from the wave branch at the same time. Its edits to the four
  shared files are one enum variant, one `mod` line, one dispatch arm, one declaration and a
  phrase in two exit-status texts. After story:protocol-floor is in the wave branch, the unit
  merges the wave branch into its branch, and its adversary pass runs on that.
- story:revision-ordering shares no file with the other two in the store's output. It adds a
  predicate form, and the new module of story:canon-mutate walks predicates: whichever of the two
  merges second merges the wave branch first and handles what the other added.

story:revision-ordering changes the language: one predicate form, `descends_from` on a case's
artifact and `item` on an evidence record, all optional. Its § First step was decided on
2026-11-20 (option A with the key `item`). The story leaves the form's name and its three-valued
table to the unit; the brief asks for both at the top of the unit's report, and the coordinator
puts them to the `sdc` session before the adversary pass.

story:canon-mutate was scoped before the protocol writer existed (story:protocol-writer, wave
7). The brief expects a mutant to be made from the parsed model and written by the writer, and
asks the unit to say so if that cannot work.
